# Product pitch: This Month

**Working name:** This Month  
**One line:** Taxfix stops being a July panic button. It becomes a two-minute monthly conversation about your life — so filing is confirmation, not archaeology.  
**Vision tagline (Tax Memory, absorbed):** Don’t remember your tax year. Build it.

---

## 60-second pitch

Germany gives people one hard tax deadline. Taxfix today absorbs all of that anxiety into a few frantic weeks. The rest of the year the product is silent — and silence is where receipts die, commutes change, and home-office days get forgotten.

We are not going to nag people back. Reminders are not value.

**This Month** is a voluntary, recurring check-in: five yes/no questions about what actually changed in your life. Job, move, home office, commute, donations, extra costs. A “yes” still marks how the month passed. A “no” is a complete answer.

Each month writes into **the calendar** — your tax year, month by month. Open the app because you want the year to be true — in control, a bit proud, a little clever — not because a badge is about to expire.

When the deadline arrives, the form is mostly filled by a year of ordinary months. Peak season gets quieter. Data gets better. Taxfix gets a reason to exist in February, June, and November.

Not a countdown. Not a streak. A habit of being finished early.

Close (optional): **Don’t remember your tax year. Build it.**

---

## The insight

People do not fail at tax because the form is hard in July. They fail because **life happened in the other eleven months** and nobody asked while it was still true.

Receipts are only one part of that story. A life event — moved, new job, WFH — can matter as much as a receipt. **This Month** captures the life context. Connecting evidence comes later.

The unit of engagement is not “tax.” It is **“what changed for you?”** Tax is the translation layer.

---

## Why this, not a better filing flow

Filing-only features fail the brief: value must exist outside season.

Notifications, fake deadlines, and streaks fail the brief: engagement without new value.

A running refund counter is tempting, but it is a number. **This Month** is a feeling: _I am the kind of person who is already done._ That is the emotional outcome the brief asked for — control, savvy, a little clever — not dutiful, anxious, or guilty.

| Feel inevitable | Of course you should capture life when it happens, not reconstruct it under deadline. |
| Real value      | Less forgotten Werbungskosten, better data, filing becomes effortless.                |
| Return loop     | Come back next month to stay caught up — not next July to panic.                      |
| Buzz            | “Taxfix asked about my life, not my forms.” Headline-shaped.                          |

---

## What we will not say on stage

- “We’ll remind you every month.” (Notification theatre.)
- “Only 247 days until the deadline.” (Fake / borrowed urgency.)
- “Keep your streak alive.” (Engagement for its own sake.)
- “Here’s the full Steuererklärung, in November.” (Filing-only.)
- “We’re an AI receipt scanner.” (Generic; OCR is deferred.)
- “Your Tax Readiness is 78%.” (Option C; not the identity.)

---

## Demo story (for later — not the product spec)

A user opens Taxfix in November. Card: **November is ready.** Five questions. They tap yes on home office and commute. The year section shows _November — home office, commute changed_. Close copy: **You’re caught up. July can wait.**

No red. No clock. No guilt.
